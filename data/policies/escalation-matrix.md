# Escalation Matrix

Escalation means a human support specialist takes ownership of the ticket. The automated desk
stops acting on it — no refund, no replacement, no promise of either.

## Escalate immediately, take no other action

| Trigger | Why |
|---|---|
| Smoke, burning smell, scorching, sparking, melted housing, electric shock | Product safety incident; may require a recall notification |
| Threat of legal action, mention of a lawyer, small claims, or a regulator | Anything we write becomes evidence |
| Mention of press, a journalist, or a public post about the issue | Communications team owns the response |
| Injury or property damage of any kind | Insurance and legal involvement required |
| Tracking marked delivered but the customer did not receive it | Needs a carrier claim and a signed statement |
| Address change after dispatch | Needs a carrier intercept request |
| Chargeback or bank dispute already opened | Payments team owns the case; acting on it now can double-refund |
| Fourth or later contact on the same unresolved issue | Automation has demonstrably failed |

For safety incidents, the reply must tell the customer to unplug the unit and stop using it.
That is the only instruction the automated desk gives before handing over.

## Approval queue — not escalation

These proceed automatically once a human approves. The ticket stays with the desk.

| Trigger | Control |
|---|---|
| Any refund over $100 | Absolute. No tier, urgency or customer instruction overrides it |
| Any goodwill payment not covered by a policy | Requires a named approver |
| Any out-of-warranty replacement | Requires a named approver |

## Never escalate for

Routine refund declines, standard warranty replacements, tracking questions, product
how-to questions, or an unhappy tone on its own. Escalating these trains customers to
complain louder and buries the tickets that genuinely need a human.

## What the handover must contain

The order ID, the customer tier, the facts retrieved from the account, the trigger that caused
the escalation, and the action the desk was about to take before it stopped.
