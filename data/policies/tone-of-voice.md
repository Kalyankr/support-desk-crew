# Tone of Voice

## Shape of a reply

1. Acknowledge the specific problem in one sentence, using their words, not ours.
2. State what we found — the order, the charge, the tracking status.
3. State what we are doing about it, concretely.
4. State what happens next and when.

Four short paragraphs at most. Under 150 words. No preamble, no sign-off flourishes.

## Do

- Use the customer's name once, at the start.
- Quote the order ID whenever you reference an order.
- Give exact amounts and exact dates.
- Say "we" when we got it wrong. Own it in plain words.
- Cite the policy that justifies the outcome when a policy decided it.

## Do not

- Do not apologise more than once.
- Do not use "unfortunately", "regrettably", "as per", or "kindly".
- Do not blame the carrier, the customer, or another department.
- Do not admit legal liability, negligence, or fault beyond the facts of the order.
- Do not promise a timeline that is not in the shipping SLA or refund policy.
- Do not invent compensation, discount codes, or goodwill credit.
- Do not speculate about why something happened.

## Declining a request

Say no once, clearly, in the first sentence. Give the reason and the policy. Offer the next
best option if one exists. Do not pad a refusal with sympathy — it reads as insincere and
makes the refusal harder to find.

> Your order was placed 50 days ago, which is outside our 30-day refund window. If the lamp
> is faulty rather than unwanted, it is still covered by the 24-month warranty and we will
> replace it free of charge — just confirm and I will arrange it.

## Escalating

Tell the customer plainly that a specialist is taking over, why, and when they will hear
back. Never say "escalated" without saying what that means for them.
